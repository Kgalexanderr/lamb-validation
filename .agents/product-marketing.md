# Product Marketing Context

**Document version:** v1
**Last updated:** 2026-10-07

## Product Overview
**One-liner:** Moss is a concept-stage wearable and companion app designed to help churchgoers stay present during sermons and revisit what mattered afterward.

**What it does:** The planned Moss Pioneer wearable would let someone deliberately record a sermon or group Bible study without taking out a phone. The companion app would organize the recording into a transcript, summary, Scripture references, notes, dates, and reminders, then let the user ask questions grounded in that personal library. Moss is not built yet; the current website is testing demand before a consumer-ready product is developed.

**Product category:** Christian wearable and sermon memory companion

**Product type:** Planned consumer hardware with companion mobile software

**Business model:** Not decided. The current conversion is a free early-access email list, with an optional Typeform survey for deeper feedback.

## Target Audience
**Target customers:** Regular churchgoers who want to remember and revisit sermons without using a phone during the service.

**Decision-makers:** Individual consumers

**Primary use case:** Record a sermon with one deliberate press, stay focused during the service, and review the message afterward.

**Jobs to be done:**
- Help me stay present during a sermon without losing what I want to remember.
- Help me find key points and Scripture after church.
- Help me revisit dates, questions, and personal reflections between Sundays.

**Secondary use cases:**
- Capture and revisit group Bible studies.
- Save personal voice reflections throughout the day.
- Remember dates and events mentioned during a sermon or study.

## Problems & Pain Points
**Core problem:** A meaningful sermon can be difficult to remember once the service ends, while taking detailed notes on a phone can pull attention away from the moment.

**Why alternatives fall short:**
- Phone note-taking can be distracting during worship.
- Handwritten notes capture fragments but often miss context.
- Generic audio recorders do not organize a message around summaries, Scripture, dates, and later questions.

**What it costs them:** Lost ideas, forgotten Scripture references, missed dates, and less opportunity to reflect on or apply the message during the week.

**Emotional tension:** People want to be fully present at church and still carry the message with them afterward.

## Competitive Landscape
**Direct:** Not yet researched.

**Secondary:** Phone note and recording apps can capture text or audio but are not designed around a churchgoer's sermon-to-reflection workflow.

**Indirect:** Paper notebooks support focused note-taking but require the listener to choose between writing and listening, and they are difficult to search later.

## Differentiation
**Key differentiators:**
- A wearable-led experience intended to keep phones out of the service.
- A personal library organized around sermons, Bible studies, Scripture, dates, and reflections.
- Questions grounded in the user's captured material and cited Scripture rather than positioning Moss as a spiritual authority.

**How we do it differently:** Moss is planned as a deliberate capture-to-reflection system: press to record, review the organized message later, and return to it between Sundays.

**Why that's better:** The user can stay present during church without accepting that the message will be forgotten.

**Why customers choose us:** This remains a hypothesis to validate through early-access signups and survey responses.

## Objections
| Objection | Response |
|-----------|----------|
| Why not use my phone? | Moss is being designed for a discreet one-press interaction so the phone can stay away during the service. |
| Is Moss available now? | Moss is a concept in development. Joining early access helps validate whether it should be built. |
| Will it record people without their knowledge? | Recording should always be deliberate, visibly indicated, and used in line with church policies and participant consent. |
| Is my spiritual life being used to train AI? | The intended direction is private-by-default content, user deletion controls, and no model training on personal recordings. These are design commitments until implemented and verified. |
| Can Moss replace Scripture, a pastor, or personal discernment? | No. Planned answers are grounded in the user's saved material and cited Scripture so the original source remains visible. |

**Anti-persona:** Someone looking for a general-purpose AI assistant, covert recorder, or substitute for pastoral guidance.

## Switching Dynamics
**Push:** Forgotten sermons, scattered notes, and the distraction of taking out a phone during church.

**Pull:** One-press capture and a searchable personal library to revisit during the week.

**Habit:** Existing phone notes, paper notebooks, or relying on memory.

**Anxiety:** Product availability, recording consent, privacy, AI accuracy, price, and whether a dedicated device is worth carrying.

## Claim Boundaries
- Always identify Moss as a concept in development.
- Describe untested hardware and software capabilities as planned features, design goals, or targets.
- Do not publish exact battery life, language count, transcription quality, or processing-speed claims without prototype evidence.
- State clearly that Moss records audio when explaining how it works.
- Do not imply that Moss can answer any question or provide spiritual authority.
- Present privacy choices as intended design commitments until they are implemented and independently verifiable.
- Encourage users to follow church recording policies and obtain appropriate consent.
